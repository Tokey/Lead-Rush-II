      02 · ANATOMY

      Six parts, one bot

      BotPerception · BotBlackboard · TacticalBotBrain · BotController · AdaptiveDifficultyTracker · TacticalChatSystem

      Click any of the six decoupled components below to inspect its exact tick rate, C# source file, internal state struct, and how it hands data to the next stage without letting bots cheat.

      02.1 · CLOCK

      What runs when

      BotController.cs · BotPerception.cs · TacticalBotBrain.cs

      A bot is six loops running at different speeds. Its hands run every frame and its eyes ten times a second. Its brain runs about four times a second, jittered by ±15 % so a squad never thinks in lockstep. Slower loops refresh the path, the adaptive profile and squad orders. Each mark below is one real tick. Press Show a sighting to watch an enemy go from the eyes to the trigger. Slow motion makes the hand-offs easy to follow.

      Regular bot; the enemy walks into view 20 m away, 25° off its aim.

      Who gives orders? The 1.5 s Orders poll runs only when a commander is plugged in: an LLM or any script that implements IBotDirectiveProvider (hollow marks show where it would ask). Out of the box none is attached, so nothing is polled and every bot decides for itself with its priority waterfall (05). The only orders come from you, and they work today with no LLM. In a team match press T, type, and press Enter. Every living teammate bot obeys at once (not on a tick), fanned out so they do not stack, for 12 s: flank left / flank right (14 m to that side and a little ahead), hold (stay on the current angle), push or flag (the capture zone, or 16 m ahead of you), and regroup / on me (back to you). Two of them answer in chat. In free-for-all and duels nobody is on your side, so nobody obeys.

      02.2 · REACT

      From in view to the first shot

      BotPerception detection meter · BotController reaction latency · Fitts flick · BotDifficulty presets

      Bots are not instant. First the detection meter has to fill. It fills faster up close, when you move or have just fired, and when you are near the middle of the bot's view. The brain's next tick then makes you the target. After that the trigger waits a human reaction time, longer when you are far off its aim, while the flick swings across.       03 · NOTICE

      The detection meter

      A bot never knows where you are just because you exist. While you are in its view cone and in line of sight, a meter fills at 0.9 + 9 / distance per second, faster if you move, much faster if you just fired. It must reach 100 % before the bot reacts. Out of sight it drains at 0.5 per second.

      04 · SEE

      Sightlines and the close arc

      BotPerception.cs · 10 Hz

      The bot looks every 0.1 s with two raycasts (HeadPosition OR ChestPosition). You are visible when inside its main vision cone, within 70 m, and unoccluded. Within 6 m, anything within ±100° half-angle (200° total arc) around its gaze counts as peripheral threat awareness. Drag the orange dot to test sightlines. Fire a shot: the bot hears it even behind cover (meter to at least 0.65, it turns to look where it heard you, off by up to 8 % of the distance); if it can also see you, the meter fills 2.2× faster for a second.

      Adaptive mode spans 0.80× to 1.38×; the presets run 0.90× (Recruit) to 1.90× (Veteran).

      05 · DECIDE

      The priority waterfall

      TacticalBotBrain.cs · about 4 Hz · BotController resupply override

      Four times a second the brain walks down this list in source order and takes the first rule that holds. Reload cover, the anti-camp flank and, since D61, pressing a weak target all happen inside Engage; Investigate still beats the flag zone. Set what the bot knows and see which rule wins and why the ones above it did not.

        06 · MOVE

        Two-layer pathfinding

        TacticalBotBrain.cs chooses where · BotController.cs drives the NavMeshAgent

        Lead Rush II splits movement into two cooperating layers: Layer 1 (TacticalBotBrain, ~4 Hz) scores candidate CoverPoint, TacticalPoint, FlagAnchor, or leader-offset world coordinates; Layer 2 (BotController, every frame) drives a decoupled NavMeshAgent (updatePosition = false) with 0.6 s / 1 m repath hysteresis, slices corners ahead on path.corners[1], blends combat ADAD strafing (0.65·route + 0.85·right·dir), completes OffMeshLinks, and triggers 1.1 s obstacle hops / 2.4 s ResetPath() stuck recovery.

        07 · TURN

        Reaction, flick and where it lands

        After the meter fills the bot waits a human reaction time, longer when you are far off to the side. Then it flicks with one fast movement whose speed peaks a third of the way in and then tails off slowly (the shape of 4,636 real mouse movements); its duration follows Fitts's law, and lands near you, not on you: errors along the flick are about 2.3 times larger than across it, and calm bots tend to land a little short.

        08 · TRACK

        Tracking a counter-strafer

        The bot sees your sideways speed through a 125 ms lag, the same lag people show, and leads by 55 ms of that lagged speed. When you change direction it keeps swinging the old way for a moment, so its misses land behind you, right after each direction change.

        10.1 · HOLD

        How the body holds the gun

        SoldierController.cs · Kinemation FPS Animation Framework · RetargetProxy.cs

        With the guard on, what stays above 60° is the detector reading the gun one frame after the aim moved (a bot turns up to about 15° a frame) on top of the 70° sprint carry. Accuracy and K/D stayed inside the match-to-match spread (12.1).

        How the cause was narrowed
        • Finger and grip edits: already reverted, flips stayed.

        • Layer ablation: only switching off the IK-motion and blending group removed every flip over 90°.
        11 · SURVIVE

        Armour and the ammo stations

        Every soldier, bot or player, has 100 health and 50 armour. Armour takes damage first and does not grow back; health does, after 4 s without damage.           12 · ADAPT

          Skill presets and the adaptive mode

          Four presets change the human limits, never the rules. The adaptive mode moves one skill value between them during the match, from how you play: your hit rate, how often you rush to close range, how often you crouch.

          13 · LEARN

          Learning your style

          AdaptiveDifficultyTracker.cs · 0.75 s refresh

          In Adaptive difficulty, bots read your live match telemetry: PlayerAccuracy01 = hits / max(8, shots), headshot ratio, crouch frequency, and rush-vs-camp tendency — which even scales the brain's anti-camp flank timer between 4.0 s (campers) and 7.2 s (rushers).

          15 · MODES

          Four modes, one brain

          MatchManager.cs · MatchSettings.cs

          Every match runs through MatchManager.cs with customizable ModeRuleConfig rules (score limit 1–100, time limit 60–1800 s, respawn delay, optional seeker drones, per-mode difficulty override) and four selectable modes from the Main Menu across all 4 maps (Saltworks, Deadsector, Foundry, Blackline).

          1v1 Duel

          TeamId.None · 1 Rival

          Head-to-head adaptive duel. First to 15 kills wins; enemyBots forced to 1, friendlyBots = 0. Bots track your individual accuracy, crouch rate, and camp timer every fight.

          Free-For-All (FFA)

          Everyone Hostile

          Up to 8 independent bots on TeamId.None fighting player and each other. Smart spawn scoring maximizes min-distance from all live threats; bots investigate third-party gunfire.

          Team Deathmatch (TDM)

          Alpha vs Bravo

          Player leads TeamId.Alpha with 0–5 friendly bots against Bravo. Friendly bots run Step 7 FollowLeader staggered 2.5–5 m wide, 4–8 m back, watching ±75° flank sectors, and answer [T] chat orders.

          21 · WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM

          Human data behind the model

          Lead Rush II uses these as calibration targets. The constants are hand-tuned to match the published behaviour; they are not fitted to a dataset inside this project.

          • Fitts (1954), J. Exp. Psychology 47(6)Movement time grows with log2(distance / target width + 1): the flick duration and why heads take longer to hit.
          • Meyer, Abrams, Kornblum, Wright and Smith (1988), Psychological Review 95(3)A fast primary movement plus corrective submovements: the flick, the 110 to 165 ms pause, then the correction.
          • Flash and Hogan (1985), J. Neuroscience 5(7)Minimum-jerk trajectories, s(u) = 10u³ − 15u⁴ + 6u⁵: the bell-shaped speed of every flick.
          • Harris and Wolpert (1998), Nature 394Signal-dependent noise: bigger, faster movements are noisier, so long flicks scatter more.
          • van Beers, Haggard and Wolpert (2004), J. Neurophysiology 91(2)Endpoint scatter is larger along the movement than across it: the 2.3 : 1 ellipse.
          • Xenopoulos and Silva (2022), ESTA: an esports trajectory and action dataset (arXiv:2209.09861); Durst et al. (2022/2024), CSKnowReal players' reaction times, crosshair placement and miss patterns used as targets: around 125 ms of visuomotor lag on a moving target, horizontal errors larger than vertical ones.
          • Listman, Tsay, Kim, Mackey and Heeger (2021), long-term motor learning in Aim LabRanges of flick times and accuracy between new and expert players, used to space the four presets.
          • MacKenzie (1992), Human-Computer Interaction 7(1)Fitts' law as a research and design tool: the log2(D / W + 1) form used for every flick time.
          • Schrum, Karpov and Miikkulainen (2012), in Believable Bots (Springer)Humanlike combat behaviour judged by people: why the bots keep human limits on reaction, aim and knowledge instead of playing perfectly.
