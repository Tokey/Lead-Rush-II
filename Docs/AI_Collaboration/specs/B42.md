# B42 - Environment realism for Lead Rush II

## What consistently makes a game environment read as real

Realism is mostly a believable relationship between scale, light, materials and
the accumulation of use. The highest-value changes are not more polygons: they
are contact cues, consistent physical light units, controlled roughness/albedo
variation, and atmospheric depth that preserves gameplay readability.

| Rank | Change for LR2 | Impact | Effort | LR2 implementation |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | Contact shadows and grounded occlusion | Very high | Low | Enable/verify HDRP contact shadows and SSAO on hero props; keep B1 visibility floor |
| 2 | Material breakup and grime masks | Very high | Medium | Add per-material variation in roughness, normal strength and dirt around seams/feet/handles |
| 3 | Decals and edge wear | High | Medium | Use the existing pooled impact/decal path for stains, tire marks, leaks and chipped edges |
| 4 | Practical lights with measured intensity | High | Low | Prefer lamps, fires and work lights that illuminate nearby geometry; use HDRP physical units and 1 Unity unit = 1 m |
| 5 | Scale cues and clutter hierarchy | High | Medium | Add repeated human-scale references, cable/pipe runs, bins, pallets and debris at believable spacing |
| 6 | Atmospheric perspective | Medium-high | Low | Use the B40 low ground layer and restrained distance fog; never obscure a soldier at 30 m |
| 7 | Wetness and weather response | Medium | Medium | Drive smoothness/darkening from the existing `WeatherSystem.Wetness`; add puddle breakup only in drainage lows |
| 8 | Colour grading and exposure consistency | Medium | Low | Keep one map-neutral grade; use local practical lights for contrast rather than crushing shadows |
| 9 | Vegetation and wind coherence | Medium | Medium | Reuse the existing foliage sway/wind signal; vary height and density at terrain transitions |

## Research notes and sources

- HDRP physical light units document real-world lux, candela and luminance
  ranges and requires the one-unit-per-metre convention:
  <https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@14.0/manual/Physical-Light-Units.html>
- Unity's HDRP feature documentation describes screen-space ambient occlusion
  as a real-time approximation of ambient-light occlusion and documents
  shadow-map update control:
  <https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition/Documentation~/HDRP-Features.md>
- HDRP decals are the appropriate bounded mechanism for local surface
  variation, stains and edge damage rather than duplicating geometry:
  <https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@14.0/manual/Decal-Projector.html>
- Physically based material authoring is useful only when scale, roughness and
  lighting are coherent; the Game Developer art discussion specifically calls
  out perspective, subtle irregular shapes and believable human proportions:
  <https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/an-approach-to-understanding-art-styles>
- Unity's lighting guidance gives practical illuminance references (for
  example overcast daylight and interior rooms), which is preferable to
  arbitrary light intensity values:
  <https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@14.0/manual/Physical-Light-Units.html>

## Reusable LR2 work delivered

- `EnvironmentRealismProfile` is the map-placeable contract for controlled
  material variation, wetness response and clutter/scale tuning.
- `AtmosphereFX` supplies the low ground layer, dust/ash motes and a reactive
  particle force field without adding a dense global fog pass.
- `SurfaceDecalPolicy` keeps surface-specific decal decisions centralized and
  compatible with the existing 128-slot impact ring.

## Gemini map-placement requests

1. Place one `AtmosphereFX` per playable map in low areas and courtyards; keep
   the mist layer 0.3-0.8 m high and verify B1's visibility checks.
2. Add a small set of contact-shadow hero props at entrances, cover corners and
   fire/light sources; do not enable expensive shadows on every clutter item.
3. Use `SurfaceDecalPolicy` variants for grime, leaks, tire marks and chipped
   edges on Foundry, Saltworks, Deadsector and TestRange.
4. Add scale/clutter passes around spawn routes and objectives, preserving
   clear traversal lanes and competitive sightlines.
5. Bind map wetness/practical-light tuning to existing weather and lighting
   builders, then capture one readable atmospheric screenshot per map.

Acceptance is a visible four-map pass with B1 readability still passing, no
new frame-time regression above the P3 threshold, and no persistent per-hit
GameObject growth.
