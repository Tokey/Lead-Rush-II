LR2 Weapon IK

How a soldier's gun gets from the animation to the screen, every system that moves it in one frame, which of them keep state from frame to frame, and the ways that lets the gun rise, spin or go wild in ADS. Built from a read of every Kinemation layer and LR2 script on the path and from test runs on 11-19 and 11-20.

NOW · 11-27

Robot driver, mirrored body: what moves the gun today

Since 11-22 the hidden driver is Kinemation's own robot (FPSGenericPlayer), with Kinemation's IK, ADS, sights and attachments. The gun hangs from IK WeaponBone under the robot's head. Since 11-23 the visible military body is posed from the robot each frame by GenericBoneMirror (LateUpdate 550); RetargetProxy no longer runs on the soldier. The sections after this one describe the 11-20 humanoid-driver setup and stay as the record of B125.

Fixed · measured

The sight arrived at 1.4-1.8× the ADS time, and kept moving

Three things stretched it. Kinemation's sine curve barely moved the gun in the first quarter of the ADS time. Its aiming motion tipped the sight 3° half way in and kept it swinging 10-17 px for 0.2 s after the gun had arrived. Out of a sprint, letting go of the keys started the 1.2 s stop lurch, which replaced the aiming motion and held the sight 50-65 px off for half a second. Now every press and release gives Kinemation's ADS layer a new curve that heads for the chosen side at full speed from where the gun is (so a tap half way never jumps; the zoom uses the same shape), the aiming motion ends within 1.5× the ADS time at 60 % size, the stop lurch is skipped while aiming, and every gun's ADS time is about 30 % shorter (AK-12 0.21 → 0.15 s).

Fixed · measured

A sight lock slid the gun out of the hands

On 11-24 a LateUpdate at 520 rotated and moved IK WeaponBone so the sight sat exactly on the camera. Kinemation had already solved the arms inside the animation graph, so the gun moved and the hands did not. Its audit measured the aim after the lock had forced it, so it could not fail. Removed: Kinemation's ADS layer (cameraBlend 1) puts the camera on the sight by itself, 0.1 to 3.9 px from the centre at 1080p on every gun and sight. In ADS the shot now follows the sight (its sway, not the cosmetic recoil kick): 0.0 to 0.3 px between bullet and sight standing, at most 2.8 px while strafing, where the sight itself swings up to 32 px. Tracers fly at 720 m/s from the muzzle's current position, so they no longer trail a strafing shooter.

Fixed · measured

Visible fingers 36-38 mm off the robot's grip

The mirror copied bone rotations through T-pose offsets, and the Mixamo thumb rests at an angle, so the visible thumb stood up over the handguard and into the iron sight. Now each palm is aligned to the robot's on two axes, every finger bone is aimed along its robot twin (0° off) and the hands are sized to the robot's: 18-19 mm, thumb out of the sight picture.

Fixed · measured

The optic came behind the eye while firing

Kinemation's demo recoil pushes the gun 5 cm back per shot, in ADS too, and the camera sits on the sight: the sight went 22 to 53 mm behind the eye. The player now uses a copy of the recoil data with 15 % of the push and 30 % of the ADS kick-back: 9.5 mm at the peak of a burst, about 5 mm in long sprays.

Restored

Kinemation core is vendor code again

Nine core files had been edited on 11-24. They are back to the vendor version plus four LR2 patches: UpdateEntity propagation, the camera parent check, the name-checked rig lookup and, new, [ReadOnly] on the left-hand chain (Unity 6.5 throws a job-safety exception without it). An A/B showed the 11-24 RebuildPlayables change did not help respawns.

WHAT WE KNOW · 11-20 10:10

Four causes, all fixed and measured

Root cause · fixed · verified

Bots' gun snapped 60 to 180° for one frame

Kinemation's IK-motion layer evaluates the motion's AnimationCurves inside the animation job, and Unity runs different soldiers' jobs on parallel worker threads. The stop, crouch, lean, jump, equip and unequip motions were shared assets, and an AnimationCurve is not safe to evaluate on two threads at once: now and then a soldier read a garbage value and its gun turned for that frame. Each soldier now plays its own copy. Without any clamp: one-frame spikes 7 and 23 → 0 and 0, largest deviation 179° and 177° → 64° and 67°.

Bug · fixed · verified

Getting shot swung the gun off the hands

The hit flinch bent the hidden driver's chest and head after the visible body had been copied, so the body never flinched but the gun, hanging from the driver's head, swung up to about 70°. It now bends the visible chest and head before the arms are solved onto the gun. Frames over 60° on Saltworks: 286 → 28 from this fix alone.

Reproduced · fixed · verified

ADS into a wall swung the rifle

Kinemation's wall tuck (25° up, 12° in, 8° roll, 6 cm back) ran at full strength in ADS, where its pivot sits at the eye. Now gated by the ADS weight (12 % left at full ADS): pressed into a wall 22.8° → 3.4° off the sight line, before death 22.9° → 3.6°.

Measured · fixed

LR2's edits fed back into Kinemation

Kinemation's ADS layer read the gun's aim point while it still carried last frame's LR2 edits; for bots the eye-plane push reached 8.2 m. The gun is now reset to Kinemation's own pose before Kinemation reads it. The B125 flip guard is off by default (-lr2FlipGuard to diagnose): with the causes fixed it would only hide a regression.

THE SETUP · 11-20

Two skeletons, one gun (the B125 setup)

Every soldier has a hidden driver: a Kinemation humanoid rig that runs all the animation, ADS, sway, recoil and IK. The gun hangs from the driver's IK WeaponBone, a child of the driver's head. The visible SWAT body copies the driver's pose each frame through Unity's muscle space, then LR2 adjusts the visible gun and solves the visible arms onto it. Nothing in the visible body feeds back into the driver, except through the gun's transform, which both share.

ONE FRAME

Everything that moves the gun, in order

Execution order in brackets. Steps in orange are the places state crosses from one frame to the next.

Update[−50]

SoldierController

  • B125: puts the gun back at its Kinemation pose (local position and rotation zero), so Kinemation never reads last frame's LR2 edits.
  • Input and look: writes MouseInput (accumulated yaw, pitch) and MouseDeltaInput for Kinemation; bots turn at up to 720°/s.
  • ADS toggle: links a runtime copy of the aiming IK motion (played at 1.8 to 3.6×; since 11-27 at 60 % size, finishing within 1.5× the ADS time) on every aim in and out.
Update[0]

FPSAnimator → FPSBoneController.UpdateController

  • Pending profile re-link: destroys all 13 layer jobs and builds new ones; each runs Initialize against the current transforms.
  • Each layer's weight from its curves and inputs (FullBodyWeight, AimingWeight, ProneWeight).
  • ADS layer reads the aim point from the gun's Transform here (before the animation of this frame).
  • Turn layer integrates the look delta into its turn angle; IK motion advances its playback.
Animationgraph

Animator, then the procedural chain

  • Locomotion clips (humanoid) write the body bones.
  • Virtual elements copy the IK hand, elbow, knee and foot targets from their bones (an absolute reset every frame).
  • The 13 Kinemation layers run in order (next section).
  • Profile blending job: after a re-link, blends every bone from a cached pose over 0.3 s.
LateUpdate[0]

Kinemation layer LateUpdates

  • Collision layer raycasts along the barrel; the hit is used in the next frame's tuck.
  • A requested profile change sets the re-link for the next Update.
LateUpdate[200]

WeaponAntiClip

  • Sets the gun's local pose to zero (the same reset as at −50, at the end of the frame).
LateUpdate[250]

RetargetProxy

  • Copies the driver pose onto the SWAT body (muscle space), aligns the hips.
  • Reload bridge: the gun follows the reload clip (clamped to ±12° pitch, ±18° yaw, ±34° cant).
  • Prone tuck, glove clearance, per-gun grip offsets (visible hand targets only).
  • Flip guard (B125): muzzle within 45° of the aim and roll within 50° (70/75 when sprinting, reloading, switching or prone).
  • Eye-plane push (B59b/B60d): keeps the gun pivot 8 cm in front of the eyes.
  • Inertial hand offsets (B60a, 45 ms half-life), then two-bone IK of the visible arms.
LateUpdate[300+]

Killcam, hit reactions, bodycam, camera shake

  • Camera and spine only; none of them writes the gun.

HOW IT BREAKS

Mechanisms, with the evidence for each

A. ADS into an obstacle confirmed · fixed

Since 11-18 19:15 (cc55b9e, D57/D59) walking no longer masks the collision layer and the tuck is 25°/12°/8°. In ADS the gun's pivot is at the eye, so the whole rifle swings. The player test reproduced it in all five variants: 22.8° head-on into a wall, 22.9° just before dying against it, 10.5° at 45°. Fix: the collision layer also takes the ADS weight as a mask (12 % left), at runtime on each weapon's profile copy and in the builder.

B. LR2 edits read back by the ADS layer confirmed · fixed

frame n: collision tuck pulls the gun back → eye-plane push p shoves the visible gun forward frame n+1: ADS reads the aim point p further forward → moves the weapon bone back by p → the gun is closer to the eyes → the push grows to p + Δ → …

The same loop carried the flip guard's rotation, the reload bridge and the prone tuck, and earlier AdsSightLock (11-19 00:42 to 03:03). Bots' eye-plane push reached 8.2 m in one bench. It does not explain the bots' raw flips: with the reset the raw weapon bone still flipped (854 and 367 frames over 60° in two runs, against 358 without).

C0. Shared motion curves on parallel animation threads root cause · fixed

frame n, worker thread 1: bot A's IkMotion job evaluates IKMotion_MoveStop.x at t = 0.31 frame n, worker thread 2: bot B's IkMotion job evaluates the same curve at t = 0.74 → the curve's segment cache is shared → one of them evaluates the wrong segment's cubic → a garbage angle → IK WeaponBone turns 60 to 180° relative to the head for that frame → next frame normal

IkMotionLayerJob.ProcessAnimation calls VectorCurve.GetValue → AnimationCurve.Evaluate on the animation thread. Unity's issue tracker: "you may evaluate a curve in a thread, but not the same curve in multiple threads". The black box caught it directly: the gun's rotation relative to the head going (289,234,153) → (59,41,21) → (289,234,153) on consecutive frames, with no change in aim, clip, speed, walls or frame time. Fix: SoldierController.OwnMotion gives each soldier its own copy of every motion (own curve objects); -lr2SharedIkMotions restores the old sharing for A/B.

C1. Hit flinch on the hidden driver fixed

HitReactions (LateUpdate 500) took its bones from the soldier's Animator, which is the hidden driver, and bent its chest and head after RetargetProxy (250) had copied the pose. The visible body never flinched; the gun, a child of the driver's head, swung up to 28° + 1.6 × 28° on a headshot. The spring now runs in Update and RetargetProxy bends the visible chest and head before solving the arms.

C. Bots' weapon bone flips inside Kinemation explained by C0 and C1

Facts: 0.6 to 7 % of bot frames, single-frame snaps up to 13,000°/s and some held states; mostly in combat ADS, about a third standing still; walls and soldiers within 1.2 m in under 10 %. The driver's animated WeaponBone stays on the soldier's facing while IK WeaponBone turns 150 to 170°, so the error enters between the PoseSampler and the end of the chain. Forcing the gun to follow WeaponBone (WeaponBoneWeight 1) did not help (0.70 % against 0.64 %).

Re-link calibration (table above) can only explain part of it: bots never switch weapons, so a bot re-links about twice per life, both at spawn (Kinemation's default profile, then the weapon's copy). A bad calibration there would leave the gun wrong for a whole life, which fits the bots that flip again and again, but not the single-frame snaps. A bench now logs every re-link per soldier, the calibration's drift and, through a black-box recorder, the 40 frames before each flip with frame time, ADS toggles, turn offset and the barrel ray, so the next run shows which kind dominates.

D. Arm spins on the visible body candidate

The two-bone IK takes its bend axis from the incoming pose. When an arm is stretched toward a far target the axis comes from the elbow hint or the target, and the elbow can flip between frames. On 11-19 00:30 the per-gun right-grip targets moved (the MK18's 8.7 cm forward); they act on the visible hands only. Likely source of the "whole arm rotating" report at 13:20.

E. ADS Euler blend amplifier

The ADS layer blends rotation through Euler angles, which jump when a component wraps. That only happens once the weapon bone is already far from the soldier's frame, so it can turn a large error into a spin but cannot start one.

DONE · 11-20

How it was closed

  1. ADS wall test after the ADS gate: every case under 4° (was 23°).
  2. Instrumented benches: no re-links and 0.0° calibration drift, which ruled the calibration theory out; the black box caught the one-frame spikes relative to the head.
  3. Shared vs own motion curves A/B on two maps without any clamp: spikes 7 and 23 → 0 and 0.
  4. Flip guard off by default; the root fixes hold without it.
  5. Still open, small: a soldier who dies while aiming respawns with the ADS weight still high and blends out over 0.2 s; the equip motion swings the gun up for about 0.8 s at each spawn (designed, from Kinemation's equip curve).
